From Middle School to Career: A National Cybersecurity Pathway Framework

A four-stage, research-based model that connects secondary and postsecondary education to high-wage, high-growth cybersecurity careers — built on stackable credentials, four flexible pathways, and alignment with Perkins V and the NICE Framework.

📈 Developmental Progression
A Four-Stage Pathway from Grade 7 to Career

The framework scaffolds learning from broad awareness to specialized, credentialed expertise, giving every student an on-ramp and an exit point with real economic value.

Stage 1 · Grades 7–10 Orientation
Orientation & Introduction
Broad exposure builds baseline digital literacy so every student can make an informed pathway choice.
  • Computer Applications for Business
  • IT Orientation
  • Security Orientation Courses
Goal: Baseline literacy & awareness
Stage 2 · Grades 10–12 Skill Development
Focused Technical Training
Multiple pathway options introduce certification-aligned coursework and marketable credentials.
  • Intro to CIS or AP CS Principles
  • Mobile App / Web Development
  • Hardware / OS Courses (IT certs)
  • Computer Science I or AP CS A
  • Intro to Networking (IT certs)
Goal: Certification-ready credentials
Stage 3 · Grade 12 Capstone
Capstone & Advanced
Advanced coursework delivers specialization and depth ahead of postsecondary entry.
  • Computer Science I & II
  • Intro to Web Development
  • AAS sequence continuation (cyber certs)
Goal: Specialization & depth
Stage 4 · Year 1 College Postsecondary
Postsecondary Courses
Recommended first-year college courses build directly on secondary preparation — no redundancy, no wasted time.
  • Seamless articulation from Stage 3
  • Direct entry into AAS/BS coursework
  • Credit for prior certifications
Goal: Continuity into a credential
🧱 Stackable Credential Architecture
Every Credential Builds Toward the Next

Credentials earned at each level contribute directly to the next, eliminating wasted time and redundant coursework — a critical barrier in traditional systems where students often repeat content when transitioning between institutions.

1

Entry Point — Industry Certifications

CompTIA A+, Network+, Security+ — earned in high school or early college.

↓ stacks into
2

Mid-Level — Associate of Applied Science (AAS)

Builds on certifications earned in Stage 1, with credit awarded for prior credentials.

↓ articulates into
3

Advanced — Bachelor of Science (BS)

Seamless articulation from AAS programs into four-year cybersecurity or CS degrees.

📊 Labor Market Alignment & Economic Impact
High-Demand, High-Wage Occupations

The framework targets 15 cybersecurity-related occupations, each meeting rigorous criteria: near or above living-wage threshold for one adult plus one child, positive 10-year growth projections (5–28%), and substantial annual Illinois job openings (90–2,690 positions). Median wages of $29.80–$65.12/hour translate to roughly $62,000–$135,000 in annual salary.

🌐 Scalability & Adaptability
Designed for National Adoption

Built on Illinois labor market data, the framework's structure travels — states can keep the developmental progression and credential architecture while substituting locally relevant career targets.

🗺️ Regional Flexibility
Accommodates state-specific occupational priorities and wage thresholds while preserving the core progression.
🤝 Institutional Collaboration
Requires coordination across secondary, community college, and four-year institutions — without prescribing a single governance model.
🚪 Multiple Entry Points
Students enter at any stage based on prior knowledge — serving traditional high schoolers and adult career-changers alike.
🧩 Modular Design
Districts with limited resources can implement one or two pathways initially, expanding as capacity develops.

✅ Conclusion

A Replicable Model for National Cybersecurity Workforce Development

The Model Programs of Study in Cybersecurity provides a comprehensive, research-based framework that successfully integrates secondary and postsecondary education with workforce needs. Its four-stage developmental progression, multiple career pathways, stackable credential architecture, and explicit competency alignment create clear routes from middle school exploration through bachelor's degree attainment and career establishment.

As a national framework, this model offers the structure and flexibility necessary for adoption across diverse state contexts while maintaining fidelity to core principles of competency development, industry alignment, and economic opportunity. Its alignment with Perkins V requirements, integration with the NICE Framework, and demonstrated focus on both technical and employability competencies position it as a replicable model that can be adapted and scaled nationally.

States seeking to strengthen cybersecurity education and career preparation would benefit significantly from studying and adapting this framework's core components to their specific regional contexts and labor market needs.
